# Design 1.2 — identity v4: separate Ed25519 and X25519 keys

> **Status:** adopted 2026-11-19 — the owner chose **option A as proposed**: the fingerprint moves to the Ed25519 key now; a verified contact stays verified after a valid continuation; an account's linked devices move together from the primary.
> **Roadmap:** `docs/ROADMAP-3.0.md` step 1.2 (review task 1.1).
> **Decided already:** the old RSA key keeps verifying for **90 days** after a contact moves, then is refused; no compatibility with P00RIJA Cryptography is kept.

## 1. What is there today (read in the code)

* One **RSA-3072** pair per account (`initializeChatIdentity`, `19-forward-secrecy.js`). It is imported both ways:
  * **RSA-OAEP** to *decrypt*: the kex contribution seal (`kex-offer`/`kex-answer`), the offline seal to the identity key (`openOfflineSeal`), the device-sync bundle, the sibling-file seal, the device beacon, the carrier wrap, the relay's old sealed challenge.
  * **RSASSA-PKCS1-v1_5** to *sign*: kex offer/answer, prekeys and their ML-KEM half, offline envelopes, space sync, profile card, relay proof (`relay-proof/1`), device sync, sibling proof, device-roster certificate, guest-mode statement.
* **The fingerprint is `SHA-256(RSA SPKI)`** and everything hangs on it:
  * the contact record (`fingerprint`, `trustedKey`, `pendingKey`, the "key changed" banner);
  * the safety number (`SHA-256(sorted fp|fp)`);
  * the relay: mailbox address, push subscription, `/push/challenge` checks `SHA-256(publicKeyData) === fingerprint`, `identityProofSigned` refuses a non-RSA key, the guest roster copy re-derives the fp from the key;
  * the device roster (`fp` field), conversation keys, history keys, the AAD binding from 1.1.
* 164 references to `publicKeyData` in `js/chat`.

## 2. The one decision that shapes everything: what the fingerprint is

| | **A. Fingerprint moves to the new key** | **B. Fingerprint stays, new keys hang off it** |
|---|---|---|
| What the fingerprint is | `SHA-256(Ed25519 public key)` | stays `SHA-256(RSA SPKI)` until the RSA key retires, then… |
| Contacts | every contact sees a new fingerprint; it must be accepted as a *continuation*, not a change | nothing visible changes for 90 days |
| Mailboxes, push, history keys | all re-keyed; mail queued under the old fp must still be fetched for 90 days | unchanged now — but must still move at the end, when RSA goes |
| End state after 90 days | clean: no RSA anywhere | the fp is a hash of a key that no longer exists; we either keep RSA forever or do A anyway |

**Recommendation: A, done once, now.** B only postpones the same migration to day 90, with less testing behind it, and leaves the identity named after a key we are trying to remove. A is what "zero backward shackles" means.

## 3. The design (option A)

### 3.1 Keys
* **Signing key** `SK`: Ed25519. Signs everything that is signed today.
* **Agreement key** `AK`: X25519. Used for every seal that is RSA-OAEP today, as **HPKE-style sealing**: ephemeral X25519 → HKDF(`poorija-seal-v4|<purpose>`, eph‖AK‖shared) → AES-256-GCM, with the purpose and both fingerprints as AAD.
* `AK` is **signed by `SK`** (`agree-key/4`), so an agreement key cannot be swapped in by the relay.
* **WebCrypto first** (`{ name: 'Ed25519' }`, `{ name: 'X25519' }`): Chrome 133/137+, Firefox 129/130+, Safari 17+/iOS 17+. Measured here: Chromium in the test rig signs (64 B) and derives (32 B); Node 26 on the relay does both. **Fallback** for an older browser: `@noble/curves` vendored into `vendor/noble-curves/`, audited, the same author as the ML-KEM we already vendor, chosen by feature detection at start.
* Private keys stay in the vault as today (encrypted store), never exported in the clear.

### 3.2 Fingerprint and the card
* **Fingerprint v4** = `SHA-256("poorija-id/4" ‖ Ed25519 public key)`, hex. The label means a v4 fp can never equal a v3 one.
* **Card v4**: `{ v: 4, fp, sk (32 B), ak (32 B), akSig, … }` — about 5× smaller than today's RSA card, so the identity QR shrinks (today 97 modules for the camera form).
* **Safety number** is computed the same way over the two v4 fingerprints. It **changes** for every contact — see 3.4 for how this is presented.

### 3.3 Moving an account (the migration)
On first start of a build with v4, after the vault opens:
1. Generate `SK`, `AK`; compute `fp4`.
2. **Cross-sign both ways** — a *continuation statement*:
   `cont/4 = { oldFp, newFp, sk, ak, at }`, signed by the **old RSA key** (proves the old owner wants this) **and** by the new `SK` (proves the new key consents).
3. Keep the RSA key in the vault, marked `retiresAt = at + 90 days`.
4. Send the statement to every contact (live and through the mailbox) and publish it on the home relay with the presence record.
5. Linked devices: the primary migrates; the others receive the v4 keys in the existing encrypted device-sync bundle (still sealed to the RSA key, which they all hold) and stop generating their own. The device roster gets a new certificate signed by `SK`.

### 3.4 What a contact sees
* A contact who holds the **pinned** old RSA key receives `cont/4`, checks the RSA signature **against the pinned key** (never against one that came with the statement) and the `SK` signature, then:
  * moves the record to `fp4` (`trustedKey` becomes `sk`), keeps `legacyFp`/`legacyKey` and `legacyUntil`;
  * shows **one quiet system line** in the conversation: “*X upgraded their security keys. Your safety number has changed because of this upgrade; it was confirmed by their previous key.*” — **not** the red “key changed” banner, and a verified contact **stays verified** (the continuation is signed by the key they verified).
* A statement whose RSA signature does not check against the pinned key is treated exactly like a key change today (banner, not trusted).
* A contact on an old build cannot read v4 and keeps talking RSA until they update; after 90 days they are told to update.

### 3.5 The 90 days
* During the window, a v4 device **accepts** RSA signatures from a contact's `legacyKey` (for mail queued before the move, signed with RSA) and still **opens** RSA seals sent to its own old key (mail written before the sender learned the new keys). It **never creates** a new RSA signature or seal toward a contact that has moved.
* After `legacyUntil`: the legacy key is deleted from the contact record and from the vault; anything RSA-only is refused with a note in the conversation.
* The relay keeps delivering mailbox mail queued under the old fp for 90 days and treats `oldFp → newFp` as an alias once it has seen a valid `cont/4` (both signatures checked on the relay, Node has both curves).

### 3.6 The relay
* `/push/challenge`, `identityProofSigned`, the hello's identity challenge and the guest roster accept a **v4 key** (`SHA-256("poorija-id/4" ‖ sk) === fp`, Ed25519 signature) beside RSA. The sealed (RSA-OAEP) challenge is not offered to v4 keys at all — the signed one already replaced it, and X25519 must never be used to open what a relay hands over.
* Mailbox alias `oldFp → newFp` for 90 days after a verified `cont/4`.
* **The relay goes first:** it ships (and is deployed to all 8 servers) one release before any client creates a v4 identity, so no client ever proves a v4 key to a relay that cannot check it.

### 3.7 What else moves to v4 in the same release
Every RSA call site is behind a handful of functions (`importIdentityPublicKey`, `importIdentityPrivateKey`, `importIdentitySigningKey`, `verifyIdentitySignature`, `kexSign`, `openOfflineSeal`, `openKexContribution`). They become version-aware: v4 key → Ed25519 / X25519 seal; legacy key → RSA, read-only after migration. The kex contribution seal (`kex-offer/4`) uses the X25519 seal; the transcript and the hybrid ML-KEM step are unchanged.

## 4. Order of work and releases

1. **2.7a — relay only:** v4 proof, alias store, `cont/4` verification. Deployed to all 8 before 2.7b. No client change.
2. **2.7b — client:** v4 keys, card, cross-signed migration, contact continuation, version-aware sign/verify/seal, the 90-day window, linked devices.
3. **+90 days — cleanup release:** RSA code becomes "refuse with a note", then is deleted.

## 5. Tests (each fails on the old code)
* `key-separation.mjs`: the signing key cannot derive, the agreement key cannot sign (WebCrypto usages), and a signature made for one purpose label does not verify for another.
* `identity-migrate.mjs`: two accounts with history and a verified contact; one upgrades. The other gets one quiet line, **no** banner, stays verified, history intact, conversation continues; mail queued before the move still opens; a `cont/4` with a forged RSA signature raises the ordinary key-change banner.
* `legacy-expiry.mjs`: clock moved 91 days: RSA signatures and seals from the old key are refused, the keys are gone from the vault.
* `relay-v4-proof.mjs`: push subscribe and hello proof with a v4 key; a v4 key whose fp does not match is refused; mailbox alias delivers old-fp mail to the new fp.
* Linked devices: `siblingsync`, `devicelink`, `deviceroster` pass with one device migrated first and the other after.
* Full regression gate on a quiet machine before each release.

## 6. Risks and what limits them
* **The biggest risk is a contact losing their contact** — a half-done migration where one side has moved the record and the other cannot be reached. Limited by: the old fp stays an alias on the relay for 90 days; records keep `legacyFp` so lookups by either fp resolve; the continuation can be re-sent.
* **Old browsers without the curves:** the noble fallback; detected once at start, recorded in diagnostics.
* **Safety number change:** presented as an upgrade confirmed by the old key (3.4), never as a key change, so people are not trained to click through real warnings.
