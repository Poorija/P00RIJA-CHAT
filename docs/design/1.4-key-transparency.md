# 1.4 Key transparency — design (release 2.10)

Status: adopted, building. Needs 1.2 (the keys logged are the v4 Ed25519 and
X25519 keys).

## What it is for

A relay hands out people's keys: in presence, in the prekey a sealed message is
made for, in the forwarding of a contact card. Every one of those is signed by
the person, so a relay cannot forge a key for an account it does not hold. What
a relay *can* do is serve a different account — a key pair it made — under a
fingerprint nobody has compared yet, or show one person a key and another person
a different one. The safety number catches that only for people who compare it.

Key transparency makes a relay commit, in public, to every key it serves:

* each relay keeps an **append-only Merkle log** of the v4 key records it has
  accepted (an account's signing key, its agreement key with the signature over
  it, and each identity move);
* it signs the **tree head** (size, root, time) with its relay key;
* relays **exchange tree heads** over the transit links they already have, and
  each keeps the heads it has seen of the others; a relay that signed two heads
  that are not one log extending the other has been caught, and it is the other
  relays — and every client that asks them — that see it;
* a client asks for an **inclusion proof** of a contact's key against a head it
  holds, and keeps the heads it has seen; a head that does not extend the last
  one it saw from that relay is shown as a warning;
* an **"unverified contact" banner** on high-risk actions (sending a large file,
  sharing the screen) until the safety number has been compared.

With eight relays, one that lies is caught by the other seven. A single-server
messenger cannot offer that.

## The log

RFC 6962 / RFC 9162 Merkle tree hash, SHA-256:

    leaf hash  = SHA-256(0x00 ‖ leaf)
    node hash  = SHA-256(0x01 ‖ left ‖ right)
    the tree of n leaves: split at the largest power of two below n

A leaf is the canonical bytes of one record:

    "poorija-kt/1" ‖ u8 type ‖ u32 len ‖ fp ‖ u32 len ‖ body ‖ u64 at

    type 1  key       body = sk ‖ ak ‖ akSig          (an account's v4 keys)
    type 2  move      body = oldFp ‖ newFp ‖ statement (a signed identity move)

All length-prefixed (u32 big-endian), so no two records share bytes. A record is
appended only when it is new: the same keys for the same fingerprint are one
leaf, ever. A relay logs a key the first time its holder proves it (the identity
challenge, sign-only for v4), never on someone else's say.

Kept in the relay's database (2.4): table `kt_leaves(idx, fp, type, leaf, at)`
and an index by fingerprint. Proofs are computed from the leaves (log n hashes
each); a cache of subtree hashes keeps it cheap past a hundred thousand leaves.

## Tree heads

    "poorija-kt-head/1" ‖ relayId(32) ‖ u64 size ‖ root(32) ‖ u64 at

signed with the relay's own P-256 key (`relay-identity`), the same key its
fingerprint is made from. A new head is signed when the log grows, at most once a
minute, and at least once an hour (so a head says "nothing changed" too).

## Endpoints (relay)

* `GET /kt/head` → the newest signed head.
* `GET /kt/proof?fp=<hex64>` → the leaves for that fingerprint with an inclusion
  proof each, against the newest head.
* `GET /kt/consistency?from=<n>&to=<m>` → a consistency proof between two sizes.
* `GET /kt/heads` → the newest head this relay holds **of each linked relay**,
  as that relay signed it (what the gossip collected).

All public, cached, rate-limited per address as the other public reads are.

## Gossip (relay ↔ relay)

Over the existing transit link (`relay-peers`), a frame `kt-head` carrying the
sender's newest signed head; sent on connect and when it changes. The receiver:

1. checks the signature against the peer's pinned relay key;
2. if it already holds a head of that relay of a different size, asks the
   sender's `/kt/consistency` for a proof between the two and checks it;
3. if two signed heads of one relay do not form one log (same size, different
   root, or a consistency proof that fails), it keeps **both** as evidence,
   marks that relay as `forked`, and reports it in its own `/kt/heads` and on
   the monitor and alert channel.

A relay never needs to believe another relay: it holds what was signed.

## Client

* On learning a contact's v4 key (import, presence, a card), the client asks the
  contact's home relay for `/kt/proof` and checks the inclusion proof against that
  relay's head. It keeps per relay the newest head it has seen and checks that
  each new one extends it (asking `/kt/consistency`).
* It also fetches its own home relay's `/kt/heads` now and then and compares the
  head of each relay there with the one it holds itself: two different views of
  one relay is the lie this exists to catch.
* What the person sees, on the contact's card and in its safety panel:
  * **logged** — the key is in the relay's log and the heads agree;
  * **not yet logged** — an older relay, or a key just made (not a warning);
  * **the relay showed two different logs** — a red line, with the evidence kept
    in the diagnostics report.
* An own check: the client asks every relay of the family for its own
  fingerprint's leaves now and then; a key there it does not hold is somebody
  having registered a key under its name, and it is told.

## The banner

Before a large file (over 10 MB) or a screen share to a contact whose
`verifiedAt` is empty, a short line above the composer: *"You have not compared
the safety number with NAME. Compare it to be sure no one is in between."* with
**Compare now** (opens the safety panel) and **Not now** (for this conversation
for a day). It does not block: it informs, once.

## What is NOT done here

* No VRF (the log is not private: a fingerprint is not secret, and the log holds
  public keys only). A later revision can hide the index with a VRF as KEYTRANS
  does.
* No monitoring service beyond the relays themselves and the clients.

## Tests

* unit `tests/unit/kt.test.mjs`: tree hash against the RFC 9162 vectors,
  inclusion and consistency proofs for every size up to 64, a forked head pair is
  detected.
* e2e `tests/e2e/keytransparency.mjs`: a key is logged on proof and only then; a
  contact's key verifies against the head; two relays gossip heads; a relay made
  to sign a different head is reported as forked by the other and by a client; the
  banner shows before a large file to an unverified contact and not to a verified
  one.
